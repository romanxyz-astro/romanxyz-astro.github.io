Mapping the three-dimensional Universe with Roman grism spectroscopy

Building on our experience with JWST imaging and spectroscopy, we use the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope to study galaxy evolution, large-scale structure, and the connection between galaxies and the intergalactic medium.

Our Science

Roman will deliver near-infrared imaging and slitless grism spectroscopy over thousands of square degrees. We use these data to study how galaxies grow and how they connect to the cosmic web and the gas around them. Members of our team lead five approved Roman Cycle 1 science programs.

Galaxy Evolution and the Cosmic Web

We use emission-line galaxies from Roman grism spectroscopy to map overdensities, filaments and protoclusters, and to test whether the cosmic web shapes galaxy evolution beyond halo mass and local density.

Resolved Galaxy Evolution

Building on our JWST work, we map stellar mass and star-formation histories within galaxies at z ~ 1–3 and ask whether their internal assembly depends on environment.

Galaxies and the Intergalactic Medium

We combine Roman galaxy samples with Subaru narrowband imaging and Rubin photometry to relate galaxies to Lyα transmission and to the neutral gas in damped Lyα absorbers.

Science Tools

We develop analysis methods and software to support our Roman science programs, building on our experience with JWST imaging and slitless spectroscopy. Our RomanXYZ grism pipeline enables spectral extraction, redshift measurements, emission-line mapping, and kinematic analysis.

We also aim to provide value-added science products and analysis tools to the broader Roman community.
